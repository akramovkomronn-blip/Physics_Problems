To find the radius of the circular path, we need to determine the velocity of the electron after it has been accelerated, then use the relationship between magnetic force and centripetal acceleration.

### 1. Find the Velocity ($v$)
When an electron is accelerated through a potential difference ($V$), its potential energy is converted into kinetic energy:
$$eV = \frac{1}{2} m_e v^2$$

Solving for $v$:
$$v = \sqrt{\frac{2eV}{m_e}}$$

Using the following values:
* Charge of electron ($e$) $\approx 1.602 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$
* Potential difference ($V$) $= 5000 \text{ V}$
* Mass of electron ($m_e$) $\approx 9.11 \times 10^{-31} \text{ kg}$

$$v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot (1.602 \times 10^{-19}) \cdot 5000}{9.11 \times 10^{-31}}}$$
**$v \approx 4.19 \times 10^7 \text{ m/s}$**

---

### 2. Find the Radius ($r$)
When the electron enters a uniform magnetic field ($B$) perpendicularly, the magnetic force ($F_B = evB$) acts as the centripetal force ($F_c = \frac{m_e v^2}{r}$):
$$evB = \frac{m_e v^2}{r}$$



Solving for $r$:
$$r = \frac{m_e v}{eB}$$

Using the given magnetic field $B = 0.1 \text{ T}$:
$$r = \frac{(9.11 \times 10^{-31}) \cdot (4.19 \times 10^7)}{(1.602 \times 10^{-19}) \cdot 0.1}$$
$$r \approx \frac{3.817 \times 10^{-23}}{1.602 \times 10^{-20}}$$
**$r \approx 2.38 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m}$** (or $2.38 \text{ mm}$)

---

### Summary of Results
* **Velocity of electron:** $4.19 \times 10^7 \text{ m/s}$ (about $14\%$ the speed of light)
* **Radius of the path:** **$2.38 \text{ mm}$**

The strong magnetic field ($0.1 \text{ T}$) coupled with the relatively high mass-to-charge ratio of the electron results in a very tight circular trajectory.
