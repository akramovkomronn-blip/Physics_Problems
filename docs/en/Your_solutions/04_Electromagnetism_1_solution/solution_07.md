First find the electron’s speed after being accelerated through the potential difference, then use circular motion in a magnetic field.

---

### Step 1: Kinetic energy from voltage



The energy gained is:


$[
qV = \frac{1}{2} m v^2
]$

For an electron:

* $( q = 1.6 \times 10^{-19} , \text{C} )$
  
* $( V = 5000 , \text{V} )$
  
* $( m = 9.11 \times 10^{-31} , \text{kg} )$




$[
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 q V}{m}}
]$




$[
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot (1.6 \times 10^{-19}) \cdot 5000}{9.11 \times 10^{-31}}}
]$




$[
v \approx 4.2 \times 10^{7} , \text{m/s}
]$



---




### Step 2: Radius of circular motion







Using the magnetic force providing centripetal force (from the Lorentz force):

$[
r = \frac{m v}{q B}
]$


Substitute values:


$[
r = \frac{(9.11 \times 10^{-31}) (4.2 \times 10^{7})}{(1.6 \times 10^{-19}) (0.1)}
]$


$[
r \approx 2.4 \times 10^{-3} , \text{m}
]$

---

### ✅ Final Answer:




**$( r \approx 2.4 \times 10^{-3} , \text{m} )$**

(or **about 2.4 mm**)

---

### Insight

A stronger magnetic field or lower speed would make the radius smaller, tightening the circular path.
