To make a proton levitate, the upward electric force ($F_e$) must exactly balance the downward gravitational force ($F_g$).

### 1. Identify the Forces
* **Gravitational Force ($F_g$):** This acts downward and is calculated as $F_g = m_p g$.
* **Electric Force ($F_e$):** This must act upward. For a positive charge like a proton, the force is in the same direction as the electric field ($E$). It is calculated as $F_e = eE$.



### 2. Set Up the Equilibrium Equation
For levitation (equilibrium), the net force must be zero:
$$F_e = F_g$$
$$eE = m_p g$$

### 3. Solve for Electric Field Strength ($E$)
Rearrange the formula to isolate $E$:
$$E = \frac{m_p g}{e}$$

### 4. Plug in the Values
Using the constants provided:
* $m_p \approx 1.67 \times 10^{-27} \text{ kg}$
* $g \approx 9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$
* $e \approx 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$

$$E = \frac{(1.67 \times 10^{-27}) (9.8)}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}}$$
$$E \approx \frac{1.6366 \times 10^{-26}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}}$$
**$E \approx 1.02 \times 10^{-7} \text{ N/C}$** (or V/m)

---

**Final Answer:**
The required electric field strength is approximately **$1.02 \times 10^{-7} \text{ N/C}$**, directed **upward**. 

Interestingly, this is an incredibly weak field. Because the mass of a proton is so small, even a tiny electric field is enough to overcome the entire planet's gravitational pull on it.
