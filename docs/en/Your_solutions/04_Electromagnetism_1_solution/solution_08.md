To find the magnitude of the force acting on the particle, we use the **Lorentz force** equation. Since the particle is moving perpendicular to the magnetic field, the electrical component of the force is not mentioned (assuming $E = 0$), and we focus on the magnetic component.

### 1. Identify the Formula
The magnitude of the magnetic force $F$ on a charge $q$ moving with velocity $v$ in a magnetic field $B$ is given by:
$$F = qvB \sin(\theta)$$
where $\theta$ is the angle between the velocity vector and the magnetic field vector.



### 2. Plug in the Given Values
* **Charge ($q$):** $2 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$
* **Velocity ($v$):** $10^6 \text{ m/s}$
* **Magnetic Field ($B$):** $0.5 \text{ T}$
* **Angle ($\theta$):** $90^\circ$ (since the movement is perpendicular to the field), and $\sin(90^\circ) = 1$.

$$F = (2 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}) \cdot (10^6 \text{ m/s}) \cdot (0.5 \text{ T}) \cdot 1$$

### 3. Calculate the Result
$$F = (2 \times 0.5) \times (10^{-19} \times 10^6)$$
$$F = 1 \times 10^{-13} \text{ N}$$

---

**Final Answer:**
The magnitude of the Lorentz force acting on the particle is **$1 \times 10^{-13} \text{ N}$**.

*Note: While this force seems small in everyday terms, for a particle with such a tiny mass ($4 \times 10^{-27} \text{ kg}$), it creates a massive acceleration of $2.5 \times 10^{13} \text{ m/s}^2$.*
