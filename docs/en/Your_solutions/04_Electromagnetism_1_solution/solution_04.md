To find the ratio between these two fundamental forces, we compare the strength of the electromagnetic interaction and the gravitational interaction at the atomic scale.

### 1. Identify the Constants
* **Charges:** $|q_e| = |q_p| \approx 1.602 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$
* **Mass of electron ($m_e$):** $\approx 9.11 \times 10^{-31} \text{ kg}$
* **Mass of proton ($m_p$):** $\approx 1.67 \times 10^{-27} \text{ kg}$
* **Distance ($r$):** $5.3 \times 10^{-11} \text{ m}$
* **Coulomb's constant ($k$):** $8.99 \times 10^9 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{C}^2$
* **Gravitational constant ($G$):** $6.67 \times 10^{-11} \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$

---

### 2. Calculate the Electric Force ($F_e$)
Using Coulomb’s Law:
$$F_e = \frac{k |q_e q_p|}{r^2}$$
$$F_e = \frac{(8.99 \times 10^9) (1.602 \times 10^{-19})^2}{(5.3 \times 10^{-11})^2}$$
**$F_e \approx 8.22 \times 10^{-8} \text{ N}$**

---

### 3. Calculate the Gravitational Force ($F_g$)
Using Newton’s Law of Universal Gravitation:
$$F_g = \frac{G m_e m_p}{r^2}$$
$$F_g = \frac{(6.67 \times 10^{-11}) (9.11 \times 10^{-31}) (1.67 \times 10^{-27})}{(5.3 \times 10^{-11})^2}$$
**$F_g \approx 3.61 \times 10^{-47} \text{ N}$**

---

### 4. Determine the Ratio ($F_e / F_g$)
To find the ratio, we can actually ignore the distance $r$ because it cancels out in the division:
$$\frac{F_e}{F_g} = \frac{k |q_e q_p|}{G m_e m_p}$$
$$\frac{F_e}{F_g} = \frac{8.22 \times 10^{-8}}{3.61 \times 10^{-47}}$$
**$\frac{F_e}{F_g} \approx 2.27 \times 10^{39}$**

---

### Summary of Results
* **Electric Force:** $8.22 \times 10^{-8} \text{ N}$
* **Gravitational Force:** $3.61 \times 10^{-47} \text{ N}$
* **Ratio:** $2.27 \times 10^{39}$

This massive ratio illustrates why gravity is considered negligible when dealing with atomic and subatomic particles—the electric force is nearly **40 orders of magnitude** stronger!
