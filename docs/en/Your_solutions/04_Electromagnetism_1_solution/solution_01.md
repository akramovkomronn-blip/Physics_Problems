### Coulomb's Law: Force at the Center of a Square

To find the net electric force on a charge at the center of a square, we calculate the individual forces from the corner charges and sum them as vectors.

#### 1. Geometry and Distance 

* **Side length ($s$):** $1.0 \text{ m}$
* 
* **Diagonal of square:** $d = s\sqrt{2} = \sqrt{2} \text{ m}$
* 
* **Distance from corner to center ($r$):** half the diagonal.
* 
    $$r = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \text{ m}$$
  
    $$r^2 = \left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{2}{4} = 0.5 \text{ m}^2$$

#### 2. Individual Force Magnitude

The magnitude of the force ($F$) exerted by one corner charge ($q_1 = +1.0 \text{ C}$) on the center charge ($q_2 = -2.0 \text{ C}$) is:

$$F = k \frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2}$$

Using Coulomb's constant $k \approx 8.99 \times 10^9 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{C}^2$:

$$F = (8.99 \times 10^9) \frac{|(1.0)(-2.0)|}{0.5}$$

$$F = (8.99 \times 10^9) \times 4 = 3.596 \times 10^{10} \text{ N}$$



#### 3. Vector Superposition

* The center charge is **negative** and all corner charges are **positive**, so all forces are attractive (pointing toward the corners).
  
* The forces from opposite corners are equal in magnitude but opposite in direction.
  
    * Force from top-left cancels the force from bottom-right.
      
    * Force from top-right cancels the force from bottom-left.

#### Final Answer
The net electric force on the center charge is **$0 \text{ N}$**. Because of the perfect symmetry of the charge distribution, the vector sum of the forces is zero.
