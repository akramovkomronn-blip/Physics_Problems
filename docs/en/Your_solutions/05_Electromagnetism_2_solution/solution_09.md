### Refraction: Snell's Law Calculation

To find the angle of refraction ($\theta_2$), we use **Snell's Law**, which relates the indices of refraction of two media to the sines of the angles of incidence and refraction.

#### 1. Formula
$$n_1 \sin(\theta_1) = n_2 \sin(\theta_2)$$

**Given Values:**
*   **Index of refraction (air), $n_1$:** $1.00$
*   **Index of refraction (glass), $n_2$:** $1.50$
*   **Angle of incidence, $\theta_1$:** $30^\circ$



---

#### 2. Calculation
Substitute the known values into the equation:
$$(1.00) \sin(30^\circ) = (1.50) \sin(\theta_2)$$

Since $\sin(30^\circ) = 0.5$:
$$0.5 = 1.50 \sin(\theta_2)$$

Solve for $\sin(\theta_2)$:
$$\sin(\theta_2) = \frac{0.5}{1.50} = \frac{1}{3} \approx 0.3333$$

Now, find the inverse sine ($\arcsin$):
$$\theta_2 = \arcsin(0.3333)$$
**$\theta_2 \approx 19.47^\circ$**

---

#### Final Answer
The angle of refraction is approximately **$19.5^\circ$**. Note that because the light is entering a denser medium ($n_2 > n_1$), the ray bends **toward the normal**, resulting in an angle smaller than the angle of incidence.
