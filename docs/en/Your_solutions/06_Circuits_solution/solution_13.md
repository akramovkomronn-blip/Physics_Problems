To find the number of turns required for the secondary coil, we use the **transformer turns ratio equation**, which relates the voltage in each coil to its number of turns.

### **The Transformer Equation**

The ratio of the secondary voltage ($V_s$) to the primary voltage ($V_p$) is equal to the ratio of the number of turns in the secondary coil ($N_s$) to the number of turns in the primary coil ($N_p$):

$$\frac{V_s}{V_p} = \frac{N_s}{N_p}$$

---

### **Step-by-Step Calculation**

**1. Identify the given values:**

* Primary Voltage ($V_p$): **120 V**
* Secondary Voltage ($V_s$): **9.0 V**
* Primary Turns ($N_p$): **400 turns**

**2. Rearrange the formula to solve for $N_s$:**


$$N_s = N_p \cdot \left( \frac{V_s}{V_p} \right)$$

**3. Substitute the values and calculate:**


$$N_s = 400 \cdot \left( \frac{9.0}{120} \right)$$

$$N_s = 400 \cdot 0.075$$

$$N_s = \mathbf{30}$$

---

### **Final Result**

The secondary coil must have **30 turns** to step the voltage down from 120 V to 9.0 V.
