To find the average current of a lightning bolt, we use the fundamental definition of electric current, which is the rate at which charge flows over time.

### **1. Identify the Given Values**

* **Charge ($Q$):** $30\,\text{C}$
* **Time ($t$):** $2\,\text{ms} = 2 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{s}$ (or $0.002\,\text{s}$)

### **2. Apply the Average Current Formula**

The formula for average current ($I_{avg}$) is:


$$I_{avg} = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta t}$$

### **3. Calculation**

Substitute the values into the equation:


$$I_{avg} = \frac{30\,\text{C}}{0.002\,\text{s}}$$

$$I_{avg} = \mathbf{15,000\,\text{A}}$$

---

### **Summary**

The average current of the lightning bolt is **15,000 Amperes** (or **15 kA**). For perspective, this is significantly higher than the current in a standard household circuit, which typically handles around $15$ to $20\,\text{A}$.
