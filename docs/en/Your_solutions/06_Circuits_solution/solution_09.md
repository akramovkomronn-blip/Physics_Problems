To find the current from a charge function, we look at the rate at which the charge is changing over time. In calculus terms, the instantaneous current $I(t)$ is the first derivative of the charge function $Q(t)$ with respect to time $t$.

### **1. The Current Formula**

The relationship between current and charge is:


$$I(t) = \frac{dQ}{dt}$$

### **2. Differentiate the Function**

Given the function:


$$Q(t) = 5t^2 + 5$$

We apply the power rule ($d/dt [at^n] = ant^{n-1}$) to find the derivative:

* The derivative of $5t^2$ is $10t$.
* The derivative of the constant $5$ is $0$.

So, the equation for current as a function of time is:


$$I(t) = 10t$$

### **3. Calculate Current at $t = 3$ s**

Now, substitute $3$ into the current equation:


$$I(3) = 10(3)$$

$$I(3) = \mathbf{30\,\text{A}}$$

---

### **Summary**

At exactly 3 seconds, the rate of charge flow through the wire is **30 Amperes**.
