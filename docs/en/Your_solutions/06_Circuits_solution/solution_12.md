To find the secondary voltage and the primary current, we use the transformer equations, which are based on the ratio of turns between the primary and secondary coils.

---

## **1. Secondary Voltage ($V_s$)**

The relationship between voltage and the number of turns is given by the transformer turns ratio formula:


$$\frac{V_s}{V_p} = \frac{N_s}{N_p}$$

**Identify the values:**

* **Primary Turns ($N_p$):** $1000$
* **Secondary Turns ($N_s$):** $200$
* **Primary Voltage ($V_p$):** $120\,\text{V}$

**Calculation:**


$$V_s = V_p \cdot \left( \frac{N_s}{N_p} \right)$$

$$V_s = 120\,\text{V} \cdot \left( \frac{200}{1000} \right)$$

$$V_s = 120\,\text{V} \cdot 0.2 = \mathbf{24\,\text{V}}$$

Since the secondary voltage is lower than the primary, this is a **step-down transformer**.

---

## **2. Primary Current ($I_p$)**

For an ideal transformer, the power input equals the power output ($P_p = P_s$). This means the current is inversely proportional to the voltage and the number of turns:


$$\frac{I_p}{I_s} = \frac{N_s}{N_p}$$

**Identify the values:**

* **Secondary Current ($I_s$):** $3\,\text{A}$

**Calculation:**


$$I_p = I_s \cdot \left( \frac{N_s}{N_p} \right)$$

$$I_p = 3\,\text{A} \cdot \left( \frac{200}{1000} \right)$$

$$I_p = 3\,\text{A} \cdot 0.2 = \mathbf{0.6\,\text{A}}$$
