To find the equation for the voltage across the resistor, we use **Ohm's Law** in its instantaneous form, which states that the voltage at any time $t$ is the product of the instantaneous current and the resistance.

### **1. Identify the Parameters**

* **Current Equation:** $I(t) = 2 \sin(120\pi t)$
* **Resistance ($R$):** $50\,\Omega$

### **2. Calculate the Voltage Equation**

In a purely resistive circuit, the voltage and current are **in phase**, meaning there is no phase shift between them. The formula is:


$$V(t) = I(t) \cdot R$$

Substituting the given current equation:


$$V(t) = [2 \sin(120\pi t)] \cdot 50$$

$$V(t) = 100 \sin(120\pi t)$$

---

### **3. Analysis of the AC Signal**

From the resulting equation, we can determine several key characteristics of the voltage:

* **Peak Voltage ($V_{peak}$):** $100\,\text{V}$
* **Angular Frequency ($\omega$):** $120\pi\,\text{rad/s}$
* **Frequency ($f$):** Since $\omega = 2\pi f$, then $f = \frac{120\pi}{2\pi} = \mathbf{60\,\text{Hz}}$
* **RMS Voltage ($V_{rms}$):** For a sine wave, $V_{rms} = \frac{V_{peak}}{\sqrt{2}} \approx \mathbf{70.7\,\text{V}}$

### **Final Equation**

The equation for the voltage $V(t)$ across the resistor is:


$$\mathbf{V(t) = 100 \sin(120\pi t)}$$
