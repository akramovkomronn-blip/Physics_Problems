To find the power dissipated and the total energy consumed, we can use the relationship between voltage, resistance, and time.

---

### **1. Power Dissipated ($P$)**

Electric power is the rate at which energy is transferred or transformed. When we know the voltage ($V$) and the resistance ($R$), we use the following version of Joule's Law:

$$P = \frac{V^2}{R}$$

**Calculation:**

* **Voltage ($V$):** $50\,\text{V}$
* **Resistance ($R$):** $100\,\Omega$

$$P = \frac{50^2}{100} = \frac{2500}{100} = \mathbf{25\,\text{W}}$$

The resistor dissipates **25 Watts** of power, usually in the form of heat.

---

### **2. Energy Consumed ($E$)**

Energy is the total amount of work done over a specific period. It is calculated by multiplying the power by the time duration.

**Calculation:**

* **Power ($P$):** $25\,\text{W}$ (which is $25\,\text{Joules per second}$)
* **Time ($t$):** $5\,\text{minutes} = 5 \times 60 = \mathbf{300\,\text{seconds}}$

$$E = P \cdot t$$

$$E = 25\,\text{W} \times 300\,\text{s} = \mathbf{7,500\,\text{J}}$$

---

### **Summary of Results**

* **Power Dissipated:** $25\,\text{W}$
* **Total Energy (Joules):** $7,500\,\text{J}$ (or $7.5\,\text{kJ}$)
* **Total Energy (Watt-hours):** Approximately $2.08\,\text{Wh}$
