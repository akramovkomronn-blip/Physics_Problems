To find the total charge and energy for capacitors in parallel, we first determine the equivalent capacitance of the system.

---

### **1. Equivalent Capacitance ($C_{eq}$)**

When capacitors are connected in **parallel**, the total capacitance is the sum of the individual capacitances because they share the same voltage and effectively increase the total surface area for charge storage.

* **Formula:** $C_{eq} = C_1 + C_2$
* **Calculation:** $4\,\mu\text{F} + 6\,\mu\text{F} = \mathbf{10\,\mu\text{F}}$ (or $10 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{F}$)

---

### **2. Total Charge Stored ($Q_{total}$)**

The total charge is the sum of the charges on each capacitor, which can be calculated using the equivalent capacitance and the battery voltage ($V = 10\,\text{V}$).

* **Formula:** $Q_{total} = C_{eq} \cdot V$
* **Calculation:** $10\,\mu\text{F} \times 10\,\text{V} = \mathbf{100\,\mu\text{C}}$ (or $10^{-4}\,\text{C}$)

---

### **3. Total Energy Stored ($U_{total}$)**

The potential energy stored in a capacitor network can be found using the equivalent capacitance and the voltage.

* **Formula:** $U = \frac{1}{2} C_{eq} V^2$
* **Calculation:**

$$U = \frac{1}{2} (10 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{F}) \times (10\,\text{V})^2$$


$$U = \frac{1}{2} (10 \times 10^{-6}) \times 100$$


$$U = 5 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{J} = \mathbf{0.5\,\text{mJ}}$$



---

### **Summary of Results**

* **Equivalent Capacitance:** $10\,\mu\text{F}$
* **Total Charge:** $100\,\mu\text{C}$
* **Total Energy:** $0.5\,\text{mJ}$
