To solve this, we use **vector addition**. To go "directly north," the boat must aim slightly **northwest** so that its westward effort cancels out the river's eastward push.

### 1. Finding the Heading Angle ($\theta$)
Imagine a right triangle where the hypotenuse is the boat's speed ($5\text{ m/s}$) and one side is the river's speed ($2\text{ m/s}$). We need to find the angle $\theta$ measured from the North direction toward the West.

* **Formula:** $\sin(\theta) = \frac{v_{\text{river}}}{v_{\text{boat}}}$
* **Calculation:** $\sin(\theta) = \frac{2}{5} = 0.4$
* **Angle:** $\theta = \arcsin(0.4) \approx \mathbf{23.6^\circ}$

**Direction:** The boat should head **$23.6^\circ$ West of North**.



---

### 2. Finding the Crossing Speed ($v_{\text{north}}$)
Even though the boat moves at $5\text{ m/s}$ through the water, some of that speed is "wasted" fighting the current. We find the actual northward speed using the Pythagorean theorem:
* **Formula:** $v_{\text{north}} = \sqrt{v_{\text{boat}}^2 - v_{\text{river}}^2}$
* **Calculation:** $v_{\text{north}} = \sqrt{5^2 - 2^2} = \sqrt{25 - 4} = \sqrt{21} \approx \mathbf{4.58\text{ m/s}}$

---

### 3. Finding the Time ($t$)
Now we use the standard formula $\text{Time} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Speed}}$ using the northward components:
* **Distance:** $200\text{ meters}$
* **Speed:** $4.58\text{ m/s}$
* **Calculation:** $t = \frac{200}{4.58} \approx \mathbf{43.7\text{ seconds}}$

### **Summary for your Professor**
You can explain the logic like this:
> "To ensure the boat travels directly north, its horizontal velocity must equal the river's speed but in the opposite direction. By using the sine function, I found the boat must aim **$23.6^\circ$ west of north**. Using the Pythagorean theorem, I determined the resulting northward velocity is approximately **$4.58\text{ m/s}$**, which means it will take about **$43.7\text{ seconds}$** to cross the $200$-meter wide river."

**Key takeaway:** If the boat just pointed straight North, it would end up downstream. By angling into the current, it "crabs" across the river to stay on a straight line.
