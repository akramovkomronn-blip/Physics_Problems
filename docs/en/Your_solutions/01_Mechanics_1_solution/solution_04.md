To find the velocity and acceleration, we use the "calculus ladder" of motion. Starting with the **position vector**, we take the derivative once to find **velocity**, and then take the derivative of that result to find **acceleration**.

### 1. Velocity Vector ($\vec{v}$)
Velocity is the rate of change of position ($\vec{v} = \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt}$). We differentiate the $i$ and $j$ components separately:
* **$x$-component ($i$):** $\frac{d}{dt}(3t^2) = 6t$
* **$y$-component ($j$):** $\frac{d}{dt}(5t - 8t^2) = 5 - 16t$

**Result:** $\vec{v}(t) = (6t)\hat{i} + (5 - 16t)\hat{j}$

---

### 2. Acceleration Vector ($\vec{a}$)
Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity ($\vec{a} = \frac{d\vec{v}}{dt}$):
* **$x$-component ($i$):** $\frac{d}{dt}(6t) = 6$
* **$y$-component ($j$):** $\frac{d}{dt}(5 - 16t) = -16$

**Result:** $\vec{a}(t) = 6\hat{i} - 16\hat{j}$
