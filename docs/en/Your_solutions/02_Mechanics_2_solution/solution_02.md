To find the spring constant and the total mechanical energy, we analyze the given equation for the mass-standing system.

### 1. Finding the Spring Constant ($k$)
The position of the mass is given by the standard simple harmonic motion equation:
$$x(t) = A \cos(\omega t)$$

From your equation $x(t) = 0.2 \cos(10\pi t)$, we can identify the following:
* **Amplitude ($A$):** $0.2 \text{ m}$
* **Angular Frequency ($\omega$):** $10\pi \text{ rad/s}$
* **Mass ($m$):** $10 \text{ kg}$

The relationship between angular frequency, mass, and the spring constant is:
$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$$

Rearranging to solve for $k$:
$$k = m \omega^2$$
$$k = 10 \times (10\pi)^2$$
$$k = 10 \times 100\pi^2$$
$$k = 1000\pi^2 \text{ N/m}$$

Using the approximation $\pi^2 \approx 9.87$:
$$k \approx \mathbf{9,869.6 \text{ N/m}}$$

---

### 2. Finding the Total Mechanical Energy ($E_{total}$)
The total mechanical energy in a simple harmonic oscillator is constant and can be calculated using the maximum potential energy (at maximum displacement $A$):

$$E_{total} = \frac{1}{2} k A^2$$

Substituting our values:
$$E_{total} = \frac{1}{2} (1000\pi^2) (0.2)^2$$
$$E_{total} = 500\pi^2 \times 0.04$$
$$E_{total} = 20\pi^2 \text{ Joules}$$

Using the approximation $\pi^2 \approx 9.87$:
$$E_{total} \approx \mathbf{197.4 \text{ J}}$$



### Summary
* **Spring Constant ($k$):** $1000\pi^2 \text{ N/m}$ (approx. $9,870 \text{ N/m}$)
* **Total Energy ($E_{total}$):** $20\pi^2 \text{ J}$ (approx. $197.4 \text{ J}$)
