To find the final speed and determine if kinetic energy is conserved, we use the principles of momentum and energy.

### 1. Find the final speed
Since the runner jumps onto the cart and they move together, this is a **perfectly inelastic collision**. We use the Law of Conservation of Momentum:

* **Mass of runner ($m_1$):** $70\text{ kg}$
* **Velocity of runner ($v_1$):** $3\text{ m/s}$
* **Mass of cart ($m_2$):** $140\text{ kg}$
* **Velocity of cart ($v_2$):** $0\text{ m/s}$ (stationary)

**Formula:**
$$m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2 = (m_1 + m_2) v_f$$

**Calculation:**
$$(70 \times 3) + (140 \times 0) = (70 + 140) \times v_f$$
$$210 = 210 \times v_f$$
$$v_f = 1\text{ m/s}$$



The final speed of the cart with the runner is **$1\text{ m/s}$**.

---

### 2. Is kinetic energy conserved?
**No, kinetic energy is not conserved.** In any inelastic collision where objects stick together, some kinetic energy is always lost. We can prove this by comparing the energy before and after:

* **Initial Kinetic Energy ($KE_i$):** $\frac{1}{2} \times 70 \times 3^2 = 315\text{ Joules}$
* **Final Kinetic Energy ($KE_f$):** $\frac{1}{2} \times 210 \times 1^2 = 105\text{ Joules}$

[Image showing kinetic energy loss in inelastic collision]

**Explanation:**
The "missing" energy ($210\text{ J}$) was converted into other forms of energy, such as **heat** (from friction between the runner's shoes and the cart), **sound**, and the internal work done to deform materials during the impact. While total energy is always conserved in the universe, *kinetic* energy specifically is lost in this type of collision.
