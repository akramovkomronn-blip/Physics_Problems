To find the speed of the pendulum bob at the bottom of its swing, we use the **Principle of Conservation of Mechanical Energy**. The potential energy at the release height is converted entirely into kinetic energy at the lowest point.

### 1. Find the Vertical Height ($h$)
The bob is released from an angle $\theta$. The vertical height $h$ relative to the bottom of the swing is determined by the length of the pendulum $L$ and the angle $\theta$:
$$h = L - L\cos(\theta) = L(1 - \cos(\theta))$$



**Given:**
* Length ($L$) = $1.0 \text{ m}$
* Angle ($\theta$) = $15^\circ$

**Calculation:**
$$h = 1.0 \cdot (1 - \cos(15^\circ))$$
$$h \approx 1.0 \cdot (1 - 0.9659)$$
$$h \approx 0.0341 \text{ m}$$

---

### 2. Solve for Velocity ($v$)
At the top, the energy is all Potential Energy ($PE$). At the bottom, it is all Kinetic Energy ($KE$):
$$PE_{top} = KE_{bottom}$$
$$mgh = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$$

The mass ($m$) cancels out, leaving:
$$v = \sqrt{2gh}$$

**Calculation:**
Taking $g \approx 9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$:
$$v = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.0341}$$
$$v = \sqrt{0.6690}$$
$$v \approx 0.818 \text{ m/s}$$

---

### **Final Answer**
The speed of the pendulum bob at the bottom of its swing is approximately **$0.82 \text{ m/s}$**.
