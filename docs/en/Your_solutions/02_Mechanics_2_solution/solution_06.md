To find the height after the second bounce, we need to track how much energy is kept after each impact.

### Step 1: Understand the Energy Loss
Mechanical energy at the top of a bounce is potential energy ($PE = mgh$). Since mass and gravity don't change, the **height is directly proportional to the energy**. 
* If the ball loses $30\%$ of its energy, it **keeps $70\%$** (or $0.7$) of its energy.
* This means after each bounce, it will reach $70\%$ of its previous height.



### Step 2: Calculate Height After Each Bounce
* **Initial Height:** $2.0 \text{ m}$
* **After 1st Bounce:** $2.0 \text{ m} \times 0.7 = \mathbf{1.4 \text{ m}}$
* **After 2nd Bounce:** $1.4 \text{ m} \times 0.7 = \mathbf{0.98 \text{ m}}$

**Final Answer:**
The tennis ball will rise to a height of **$0.98 \text{ m}$** after the second bounce.
