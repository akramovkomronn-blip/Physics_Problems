To find the period of the pendulum on the Moon, we look at the relationship between gravity and time in a pendulum system.

### The Physics
The period of a simple pendulum ($T$) is defined by the formula:

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}$$

* **$L$** is the length of the string (which remains constant).
* **$g$** is the acceleration due to gravity.

### The Calculation
Since the length doesn't change, the period is inversely proportional to the square root of gravity ($T \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}$).

1.  **Gravity Change:** The Moon's gravity is $\frac{1}{6}$ of Earth's. 
2.  **Period Change:** Because gravity is in the denominator under a square root, the period will increase by a factor of $\sqrt{6}$.
3.  **Final Result:**
    $$T_{moon} = 4 \times \sqrt{6}$$
    $$T_{moon} \approx 4 \times 2.449$$
    $$T_{moon} \approx 9.8 \text{ seconds}$$



---

**Simple Summary:**
On the Moon, the pendulum will swing much slower because the "pull" of gravity is weaker. It will take approximately **9.8 seconds** to complete one full swing.
