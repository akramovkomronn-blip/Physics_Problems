---

### 1. Pendulum on the Moon
Since the length $L$ of the pendulum doesn't change, the period is inversely proportional to the square root of gravity ($T \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}$).

* **Gravity Change:** Gravity on the Moon ($g_{Moon}$) is $\frac{1}{6}$ of Earth's gravity ($g_{Earth}$).
* **The Logic:** If gravity decreases by a factor of 6, the period increases by a factor of $\sqrt{6}$.
* **Calculation:**
    $$T_{Moon} = T_{Earth} \times \sqrt{6}$$
    $$T_{Moon} = 4 \times 2.449 \approx \mathbf{9.80 \text{ seconds}}$$

**The "Simple" Why:** Because the Moon's gravity is weaker, it pulls the pendulum back to the center much more slowly, making the swing take longer.

---

### 2. Required Length for a 1-Second Period on Earth
To find the specific length $L$ needed for a period $T = 1$ second on Earth (where $g \approx 9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$), we rearrange the formula:

1.  **Square both sides:** $T^2 = 4\pi^2 \frac{L}{g}$
2.  **Solve for L:** $L = \frac{g T^2}{4\pi^2}$
3.  **Plug in the numbers:**
    $$L = \frac{9.8 \times (1)^2}{4\pi^2}$$
    $$L = \frac{9.8}{39.478}$$
    $$L \approx \mathbf{0.248 \text{ meters (or 24.8 cm)}}$$



### Summary
* **Period on the Moon:** ~9.8 seconds
* **Required Length on Earth:** ~0.248 meters (roughly 25 cm)
