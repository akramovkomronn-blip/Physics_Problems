To find the period of the pendulum on the Moon, we use the formula for the period of a simple pendulum:

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}$$

Where:
* $T$ is the period.
* $L$ is the length of the pendulum.
* $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity.

### Step-by-Step Calculation:

1.  **Identify the relationship:** Since the length ($L$) of the pendulum stays the same, the period is inversely proportional to the square root of gravity ($\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}$).
2.  **Compare Moon and Earth:** Gravity on the Moon ($g_{Moon}$) is $1/6$ of Earth's gravity ($g_{Earth}$).
3.  **Apply the change:** When gravity decreases by a factor of $6$, the period increases by a factor of $\sqrt{6}$.
    $$T_{Moon} = T_{Earth} \times \sqrt{6}$$
4.  **Calculate:** $$T_{Moon} = 4 \text{ seconds} \times \sqrt{6}$$
    $$T_{Moon} \approx 4 \times 2.45$$
    $$T_{Moon} \approx 9.8 \text{ seconds}$$



**Simple Answer:**
The period of the pendulum on the Moon would be approximately **9.8 seconds**. Because gravity is weaker on the Moon, the pendulum swings more slowly, taking more time to complete one full cycle.
