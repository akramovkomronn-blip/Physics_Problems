Let’s use the simple pendulum formula:

[
T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}
]

---

### **1. Period on the Moon**

For the same pendulum length (L), the period depends on gravity:

[
T \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}
]

On the Moon:
[
g_{\text{moon}} = \frac{g_{\text{earth}}}{6}
]

So the new period is:

[
T_{\text{moon}} = T_{\text{earth}} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{g_{\text{earth}}}{g_{\text{moon}}}} = 4 \cdot \sqrt{6}
]

[
T_{\text{moon}} \approx 4 \cdot 2.45 \approx 9.8 \text{ s}
]

**Answer:** ≈ **9.8 seconds**

---

### **2. Length for a 1-second pendulum (on Earth)**

Rearrange the formula:

[
L = \frac{gT^2}{4\pi^2}
]

Using:

* (T = 1) s
* (g \approx 9.81 , \text{m/s}^2)

[
L = \frac{9.81 \cdot 1^2}{4\pi^2} \approx \frac{9.81}{39.48} \approx 0.248 \text{ m}
]

**Answer:** ≈ **0.248 m** (about **24.8 cm**)

---

### ✅ Final Answers:

* **Period on the Moon:** ~**9.8 s**
* **Required length for 1 s period:** ~**0.248 m**
